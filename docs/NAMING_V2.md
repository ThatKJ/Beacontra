# Naming V2 — Fresh Candidate Universe

**Why this exists:** the V1 finalists (Vantle, Marqline, Glintra) were reopened on new collision evidence (per the user, an active `vantle.org`/GitHub "Vantle" open-source project — my own quick spot-check found a real but differently-shaped collision than described, an active "Vantel" AI product spelled close enough to confuse, and Marqline/Glintra collisions I have not independently re-verified but am not disputing). Rejecting all three per instruction — sunk cost from V1's research effort is not a reason to keep a name with real collision risk. This is a genuinely fresh 30, avoiding every name that appeared anywhere in `docs/NAMING.md` (survivors and eliminated alike), leaning harder into invented/blended words per the explicit instruction to prioritize brandability over dictionary mashups.

**My own quick check on the V1 rejection basis (transparency, not re-litigating the decision):** searched "vantle.ai" directly — found `vantle.org`/`github.com/Vantle/Vantle` ("Platform for everything," appears to be a small open-source project, not exactly the "AI/e-learning coursework platform" described) and a separately-spelled "Vantel" (a real, active AI product for commercial insurance brokers). The collision is real in substance (a "Vantle"-branded open-source project exists) even if the specific characterization differs in detail — the rejection stands either way. I have not independently re-verified the Marqline/Glintra claims; treating those as given per instruction rather than spending further time on names we're not using regardless.

## 30 fresh candidates

**1. Veyra** — VAY-rah. Invented; soft, no fixed meaning, narratively anchorable to clarity/truth without using "Veri-". Fit: neutral (blank-slate). Risk: generic-pleasant, could suit almost any industry — meaning has to be taught. Memorability: medium-high. Demo: smooth, easy to say.

**2. Quovant** — KWOH-vant. Invented, evokes "quo" (query/inquiry, as in status quo) + "vantage." Fit: good — inquiry + vantage point over the marketplace. Risk: could be misheard as a Latin-legal term, faint "quo warranto" legal-proceeding association. Memorability: high, distinctive. Demo: strong, sounds like a real enterprise SaaS.

**3. Lumeric** — loo-MEHR-ik. "Lume" (light, as in illuminate/reveal) + brandable suffix. Fit: decent visual metaphor without saying Lens/Vision. Risk: close enough to "numeric"/"turmeric" to mishear; could sound like a lighting or spice brand. Memorability: medium. Demo: pleasant but slightly odd fit.

**4. Rivelio** — rih-VEL-ee-oh. Echoes Italian "rivelare" (to reveal). Fit: strong thematically (revealing what's really behind a listing). Risk: could scan as a fashion/luxury brand given the Italian flavor. Memorability: high — distinctive rhythm. Demo: elegant, works well said aloud.

**5. Onvexa** — on-VEK-sah. Fully invented. Fit: none inherent — blank slate. Risk: same as Veyra, no anchor to the product. Memorability: medium. Demo: fine, forgettable without context.

**6. Quorvia** — KWOR-vee-ah. Invented, evokes "quorum" (a body of evidence/consensus) + suffix. Fit: decent — "quorum of evidence" is close to the actual mechanism (multiple signals fused). Risk: quorum's governance/legal connotation could imply more procedural certainty than the product offers. Memorability: medium-high. Demo: sounds serious and credible.

**7. Meridex** — MEH-rih-deks. "Meridian" (a reference line/standard) + "index" (a catalog). Fit: good — the product is essentially an index of listings measured against a standard (the official photo). Risk: "-dex" suffix pattern is common in data/analytics naming, slight genericness. Memorability: medium. Demo: sounds like a real analytics company.

**8. Solvane** — SOL-vayn. "Solve" + "vane" (a directional indicator/weathervane). Fit: decent — indicates direction/answer. Risk: "solve" implies more resolution/certainty than the product claims to offer. Memorability: medium. Demo: fine.

**9. Kestrion** — KEH-stree-on. Echoes "kestrel" (a sharp-eyed bird of prey — sharp observation, not aggression). Fit: decent metaphor (sharp-eyed watching) without using "Watch"/"Guard" literally. Risk: could sound fantasy/gaming-brand-flavored rather than enterprise B2B. Memorability: high — distinctive. Demo: strong, memorable, slightly unusual to say the first time.

**10. Arvexa** — ar-VEK-sah. Fully invented. Fit: none inherent. Risk: sounds pharmaceutical (close to real drug-name patterns like "Farxiga," "Arvekta"). Memorability: medium. Demo: fine but wrong-industry association.

**11. Beacontra** — bee-KON-trah. "Beacon" (a signal/alert, non-accusatory) + brandable ending. Fit: strong — a beacon signals attention without asserting guilt. Risk: slightly long (4 syllables); "-tra" ending pattern recurs a lot in this list, worth varying more. Memorability: medium-high. Demo: decent, a bit of a mouthful.

**12. Solstra** — SOL-strah. "Sol" (clarity/sun) + brandable ending. Fit: weak-moderate, mostly aesthetic. Risk: sounds like a solar-energy or wellness brand. Memorability: medium. Demo: pleasant, wrong-industry lean.

**13. Ferravo** — feh-RAH-voh. Echoes "ferrum" (iron — strength/reliability) without being a literal word. Fit: weak on meaning, strong on sound (serious, sturdy). Risk: could read as an automotive/industrial brand (close to "Ferrari" cadence). Memorability: high. Demo: strong, confident sound.

**14. Trelio** — TREH-lee-oh. Invented, smooth, short. Fit: none inherent. Risk: close to "trellis"/"folio" — mild real-word adjacency, low risk. Memorability: medium-high. Demo: easy, pleasant.

**15. Novantra** — no-VAN-trah. "Nova" (new) + brandable ending. Fit: weak — generic "new/innovative" framing, doesn't name the mechanism. Risk: "Nova-" is one of the most overused prefixes in tech naming — real saturation concern. Memorability: medium. Demo: fine but forgettable among many similar-sounding "Nova-" products.

**16. Verascan** — VEH-rah-scan. "Vera" (true) + "scan" (an explicitly encouraged concept per the brief). Fit: strong, names both the truth-checking goal and the literal action (scanning listings). **Flagging myself:** "Vera-" prefix is heavily saturated in the verification-SaaS category specifically (same issue that eliminated Veridex/Verolist in V1) — likely elimination candidate despite good meaning-fit.

**17. Halovex** — HAY-loh-veks. "Halo" (a highlighting ring/marker around something notable) + brandable ending. Fit: decent — a halo highlights without accusing. Risk: "halo effect" has an unrelated, mildly negative connotation in marketing/psychology (a halo can mean unwarranted positive bias) — minor risk, worth knowing. Memorability: high. Demo: strong, distinctive.

**18. Kelvara** — kel-VAR-ah. Fully invented, smooth. Fit: none inherent. Risk: sounds skincare/wellness-adjacent (similar issue to several "-ara"/"-vara" names). Memorability: medium. Demo: pleasant, wrong-industry lean.

**19. Onwyra** — on-WY-rah. Fully invented. Fit: none. Risk: hard to guess spelling from sound (the "wy" is unusual) — real risk for a name people need to type into a browser/GitHub search. Memorability: medium (sound), low (spelling recall). Demo: fine spoken, risky written.

**20. Traversa** — truh-VER-sah. From "traverse" (to cross/move across — traversing the marketplace landscape to find listings). Fit: strong, real-word root with a fresh, unused-so-far meaning-tie for this project. Risk: "traverse" is used across mapping/logistics/climbing-gear branding — moderate genericness in unrelated industries, low risk of confusion within ours. Memorability: high. Demo: strong, sounds premium and precise.

**21. Verrant** — VEH-rant. Blends "vera" (true) + "errant" (wandering — like a stray/reused photo wandering to the wrong listing) — or reads close to "warrant." Fit: interesting double meaning. **Flagging myself:** the "warrant" echo risks implying legal certainty (the same problem that killed Proofmark in V1) — likely elimination candidate on this basis alone regardless of collision status.

**22. Faelix** — FAY-liks. Invented, soft, short. Fit: none inherent. Risk: close to "Felix" (a common personal name/brand, e.g. Felix pet food) — mild collision-adjacent risk, worth a real check if it survives. Memorability: medium-high. Demo: pleasant.

**23. Sourvex** — SOOR-veks. "Source" (an explicitly encouraged concept word) + brandable ending. Fit: good — the product traces a listing back to whether it matches the real source. Risk: "sour" as the first syllable has an unfortunate negative-sounding lead-in despite the intended "source" reading. Memorability: medium. Demo: risk of the "sour" misreading landing oddly in a live pitch.

**24. Vantify** — VAN-tih-fy. "Vantage" + "-ify." **Flagging myself:** the "-ify" suffix is one of the most worn-out SaaS naming clichés (same issue that weakened Attestly in V1) — elimination candidate on construction pattern alone, meaning-fit aside.

**25. Truvane** — TROO-vayn. "True" + "vane" (indicator). **Flagging myself:** "Tru-" prefix is another heavily saturated pattern (TrueShelf already used this root in V1; broader industry has many "Tru-" brands) — elimination candidate for genericness.

**26. Cintel** — SIN-tel. **Flagging myself immediately:** reads too close to "Intel"/"intelligence," which sits right next to the explicitly excluded "Intelli-" pattern in spirit even though it's not the literal word. Elimination candidate on instruction-compliance grounds.

**27. Kortexa** — kor-TEK-sah. **Flagging myself immediately:** echoes "cortex" (brain) — reads as generic-AI-brand-speak, the exact "AI slop" register the brief explicitly warns against. Elimination candidate.

**28. Aurelix** — ow-REH-liks. "Aurel-" (gold/aura, premium connotation) + brandable ending. Fit: weak-moderate, mostly aesthetic/premium-signaling rather than descriptive. Risk: could sound like a jewelry or finance brand. Memorability: high. Demo: strong, confident sound.

**29. Nexvane** — NEKS-vayn. "Nex" (next/nexus — a connecting point across marketplaces) + "vane" (indicator). Fit: decent — a nexus point reading multiple signals. Risk: "Nex-" prefix is common in telecom/tech naming (Nexus, Nextel echo) — moderate genericness. Memorability: medium. Demo: fine.

**30. Coravex** — kor-AH-veks. Fully invented, serious/enterprise-sounding, ends on a hard consonant like several of the stronger names above. Fit: none inherent — blank slate. Risk: same trade-off as Veyra/Onvexa/Trelio — needs the meaning taught. Memorability: high. Demo: strong, sounds like a real funded startup.

## First elimination (Phase 2)

Eliminated on construction/compliance/self-flagged grounds, not just taste:
- **Cintel, Kortexa** — read as AI-brand-cliché / too close to the explicitly excluded "Intelli-" pattern.
- **Vantify, Truvane** — worn SaaS-suffix and "Tru-"-prefix clichés respectively.
- **Verrant, Verascan** — Verrant risks implying legal certainty (the Proofmark problem); Verascan is meaning-strong but sits in the saturated "Vera-" naming cluster for this exact industry.
- **Solstra, Kelvara, Arvexa** — wrong-industry sound associations (wellness/pharma) strong enough to be a real first-impression risk.
