# Naming Decision — FINAL

## FINAL NAME

**Beacontra**

## PRONUNCIATION

bee-KON-trah (4 syllables, stress on the second)

## MEANING

"Beacon" (a signal that draws attention to something, without asserting a verdict about it — a lighthouse beacon warns, it doesn't convict) + a brandable ending. A beacon is the right metaphor for a tool whose entire design philosophy, established over this whole project, is "flag for human review," never "confirmed counterfeit."

## SEARCHES PERFORMED

Two independent passes, converging:
- **CLAUDE** (`docs/NAMING_V2.md`): direct single-name web search — no material collision found (only unrelated "BeaconTrax," a differently-spelled IoT tracking company, surfaced as the nearest match).
- **GEMINI** (`docs/NAMING_AUDIT_V2.md`): independent, broader pass across software/AI/GitHub/startup/e-commerce/India-specific surfaces — "No software, startup, or GitHub repo found... Very safe." Search noise was limited to unrelated "beacon transmission" (aerospace) and "contra" (medical) terminology, not a naming collision.
- Neither search constitutes formal trademark clearance — both are practical hackathon-scoped naming research, consistent with the instruction not to claim "available" or "trademark clear."

## KNOWN COLLISIONS

None found by either independent search pass. The closest tangential hit ("BeaconTrax," an IoT Bluetooth-tracking company) is a different spelling, a different industry, and not a software/SaaS product in our space — negligible confusion risk.

## WHY COLLISIONS ARE NOT MATERIAL

There are none to weigh — this is the cleanest result across both the V1 and V2 naming rounds, confirmed independently twice.

## WHY IT FITS THE PRODUCT

1. **Directly matches the product's core design commitment.** Every major decision this session (the language change away from "counterfeit detector," the three-state evidence model in T-026, the "evidence for review, not accusation" framing throughout `docs/DECISION.md`/`docs/COMPETITIVE_ADJUDICATION.md`) has been about making sure the product signals risk without asserting a verdict. "Beacon" is the one metaphor in the entire candidate pool that names this exact posture precisely, not just visually or mechanically.
2. **Future-extensible, not mechanism-locked.** A beacon can signal on *any* basis — price, seller, visual evidence, or a future signal type not yet built — without needing to be renamed if the product's signal mix shifts. This matters given the roadmap already includes P1 features (`docs/PRODUCT_SPEC.md`) beyond the current three signals.
3. **Professional, B2B-credible tone.** Reads as a serious enterprise tool, not a consumer app or a security-vendor cliché — consistent with the brief's explicit instruction to sound like "an actual polished startup."
4. **Demo-quality:** "Beacontra flagged three listings requiring review" is a natural sentence a presenter can say without stumbling, and it reads well as a browser `<title>` and GitHub repo name.

## WHY RUNNER-UP #1 (Glarevex) LOST

Also fully clean on collision (confirmed by both independent searches) and the punchiest/shortest of the three finalists, with a strong, precise visual metaphor (glare/reflection off a photo, tying directly to the reverse-image mechanism). It lost on two points applied from the pre-committed rubric (`docs/NAMING_V2.md`'s Phase 5 rubric): first, **tone risk** — "glare" carries a mild everyday connotation of hostility ("to glare at someone"), which sits in tension with the carefully-built non-accusatory brand positioning that "Beacon" avoids entirely. Second, **narrower future-extensibility** — its meaning ties specifically to the visual/photo signal, which is apt today but less apt if the product's signal mix evolves, whereas "Beacon" stays accurate regardless of which signal is doing the flagging.

## WHY RUNNER-UP #2 (Ferravo) LOST

The cleanest collision result of the entire V1+V2 process (genuinely zero relevant hits in either independent search) and a confident, professional sound. It lost on **product relevance**: it is a pure blank-slate name (echoing "ferrum"/iron only abstractly) with no inherent tie to what the product actually does, meaning the "why is it called that" story has to be entirely taught rather than partially self-evident — a real cost against Beacontra and Glarevex, both of which carry an immediate, explicable metaphor. Also noted for completeness (per direct instruction, not disqualifying on its own): Ferravo is not a fully invented string — it is the first-person-singular imperfect indicative form of the Italian verb *ferrare* ("to shoe a horse"), confirmed via Wiktionary — a minor oddity that tipped a close decision further in Beacontra's favor given the two were otherwise close on collision-cleanliness.

## RISKS ACCEPTED

- **Meaning still requires a one-sentence explanation** ("it's a beacon — it signals for review, it doesn't accuse") — not fully self-evident from the word alone, but this is true of nearly every strong startup name (Stripe, Notion, Vercel all require the same kind of one-line explanation) and is a smaller lift than a fully blank-slate name like Ferravo would require.
- **Not a formal trademark clearance** — both collision passes were practical web/software-surface searches, not a legal search. Acceptable for a hackathon submission per the brief's own explicit instruction not to claim legal availability.
- **Four syllables** is on the longer side versus Glarevex — a real but minor demo-fluency cost, outweighed by the stronger meaning-fit and tone-safety above.
